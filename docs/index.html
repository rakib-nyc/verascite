Independent research  /  Apache 2.0  /  not legal advice

A citation is an address. Verification means going there.

550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007) is a coordinate: volume, reporter, page, pincite, year. Every part of it can be checked independently against the published record — and every part can be wrong on its own. VeraScite checks each one and shows you what it found.

*545Failure modes

Two problems that look identical on the page

Both citations below are correctly formatted. One names a reporter series that has never been published in the United States. The other is a real decision that simply has not reached free archives. A reviewer reading for form cannot tell them apart — and neither can a tool that only asks whether it found a match.

FLAGGED

The reporter abbreviation does not appear in the catalogue of every reporter recognised in American law. This is an affirmative finding, backed by evidence.

Smith v. Fictional Reporter Co.,
    88 Jurisprudentia 100 (9th Cir. 2018)
UNVERIFIED

A vendor-only identifier for a real decision. Absent from free archives as a matter of course. Reporting this as fabrication would be a false accusation.

Tinch v. Video Indus. Servs., Inc.,
    2019 WL 1396975 (E.D. Mich. 2019)

Invented cases also get the attention they deserve — but they are the minority. The larger share of defects are real, published, verifiable decisions cited for something they do not hold, or quoted inaccurately, or pincited to the wrong page. Those survive every check short of reading the opinion.

*546Verdicts

Absence of evidence is never evidence of fabrication.

If VeraScite cannot find a case, it reports UNVERIFIED and names the sources it searched. It never reports that the case is fake. There is no FABRICATED verdict anywhere in the codebase, by design.

Telling an attorney that a sound citation is fabricated costs more than staying quiet about a defect. So the two states are kept structurally apart — including in their colour: absence is rendered in neutral slate, never in warning amber, because absence is not a warning.

ResultWhat it meansWhat it does not mean
FLAGGED A retrieved source contradicts the document. Evidence attached.—
UNVERIFIED Absent from the sources consulted. Check by hand. Not a finding of fabrication.
REVIEW Something needs a human read.Not a failure.
VERIFIED Confirmed on every dimension checked. Not a statement that it is still good law.
*547Abstention

The number that decides whether a checker is usable

About one citation in ten in a real appellate brief is absent from the free archives — not fabricated, not defective, simply not held. Recent decisions, unpublished dispositions, state trial courts and vendor-only identifiers go missing as a matter of routine.

So the question is not how much a checker catches. It is what it does with a citation it cannot find. A tool that answers that badly does not fail occasionally — it accuses sound authority a tenth of the time.

SystemTest setSound citations called fabricated
Gemini132 real-but-absent65.9%
GPT-5132 real-but-absent25%
VeraScite95 unreachable, of 996 sound citations0%
VeraScite101 absent-from-all-sources control0%
VeraScite149 vendor-only, court-adjudicated fabricated0%

Zero in 345, across three independent samples

The last row is the strongest and the least intuitive. Those citations really were fabricated — a court said so in an order. The tool still declined to say so, because a vendor-only identifier that was invented is indistinguishable from one naming a real unreported decision when no free source holds either.

Refusing to guess there is the same behaviour that produces the zeros above, seen from the opposite direction.

And it tells you your own figure

Measured 9.5% unreachable on that corpus — independently corroborating the ~10.2% reported in the published work. Every run now states its own coverage before the counts, so “nothing found” can never be read as “nothing there”.

11 citation(s) in this document are absent from
the sources consulted and were not examined.
That reflects what the free archives hold, not
the quality of those citations.

Comparison rows are published benchmark results on a different test set — comparable in task, not item for item. Running this harness over those exact 132 citations is the obvious next step and has not been done. Full protocol and limits are in the repository.

*548Reach

Two surfaces, for the two populations that actually need this

People representing themselves

Of the US filings in which a court has found a fabricated citation, roughly six in ten were filed by someone with no lawyer — no firm, no research subscription, and usually no idea that the tool which drafted their filing can invent authority that does not exist. They are the largest affected population and the one least served by anything in this field.

$ verascite my-motion.docx --out ./check --plain

The workflow guide walks through checking citations from an AI-assisted research session, for people working without a paid subscription.

The same findings, without vocabulary anyone has to look up — and taking particular care in the direction that matters:

This does not mean it is fake. Many real cases are not in free archives — recent ones, unpublished ones, state trial courts, and cases only paid services carry.

The systems that write the citations

The useful place to check a citation is inside the drafting tool, before anything reaches a filing — not in a separate application someone has to remember to open afterwards.

$ python -m verascite.mcp_server

There is also a Word add-in, which checks the citations in a draft and leaves a comment in the margin on each finding — and on nothing that is merely absent from an archive.

Free lookup endpoints already answer “is there a case at this citation.” What they do not do is decline properly. This returns the three-state answer — contradicted / unverified / confirmed — and ships the reporting constraint with every response, because a calling model paraphrases whatever it is handed, and NOT_FOUND becomes “this case does not exist” in about three hops.

*549Reading

The half of the tool users could not reach

Deciding whether an opinion actually supports the proposition it was cited for requires reading the opinion. That layer existed, was tested, and was unreachable from the installed command line. Every user got the deterministic half — 37.3 F1 instead of roughly 70 — and content misrepresentation, the largest single class of citation defect, was 0% detectable.

It now runs from a flag, against a backend you choose.

$ verascite brief.docx --out ./audit \
    --model local --model-name YOUR-MODEL

reading   21 citations against their opinions
          local backend, nothing left this machine

Nothing leaves the machine, and that is enforced

--model local refuses any endpoint that is not a loopback address. For a lawyer holding privileged material, the guarantee that no citation, proposition, or opinion text leaves the machine cannot be a sentence in a manual. It is a check in the code, and a test that fails if it is removed.

Three backends: a loopback endpoint, any hosted endpoint speaking the chat‑completions JSON interface, or a plain subprocess reading a prompt on stdin. No vendor SDK, no vendor's option table baked in.

A reading is labelled as a reading

The deterministic layer is reproducible by construction. A model is not. So every verdict a model touched is marked in the report, next to the backend, the model, whether text left the machine, what the run cost, and a plain statement that a different model may read differently.

--deterministic-only remains the default. Nothing reads unless you ask it to.

And because nobody reviews one brief at a time, --batch takes a matter folder and returns one consolidated report, ordered most‑severe‑first. A file that cannot be read is named as unexamined, never as clean.

Which backend you choose changes the result, and the effect is large.

Measured on the same samples, a small 4 B local model reached 5.6% recall against 82.7% for the reader used in the evaluation. It answers; almost every answer is low‑confidence and reaches you as “a human should look at this.”

The part worth keeping: under a weak reader the tool degrades toward silence, not toward false accusation — one false positive in 35, and the span interlock caught a further one the model was about to make. A small local model is a privacy floor, not a capability floor. Measure your own; the harness that produced these numbers ships with the tool.

*550Record

The artifact a standing order actually contemplates

113 active standing orders now govern AI use in filings. The requirement they have converged on is not disclosure. It is certification that a licensed attorney independently verified every citation — and sanctions attach to the failure to verify, not to the use of a drafting tool.

A record of inquiry, not a clearance

Every run writes verification‑record.md. It binds itself to a SHA‑256 of the exact file reviewed, so it cannot be re‑attached to a later draft. It states the tool version and the threshold set. It lists every citation with the sources consulted for it and when they were retrieved.

Then it enumerates what was not examined — good‑law status first, and before the results, because a list of confirmations read without its caveats is a misleading record.

The attorney's attestation block is left blank. It has to be: a record that arrived pre‑filled would be a certification this tool has no standing to make.

Statutes, and the subsection that was never there

Statutory citations used to be out of scope entirely — seen, counted, set aside. They are now checked against free federal sources that need no credential, including the official structural text of the US Code.

Which makes one particular finding possible:

→ 42 U.S.C. § 1983(a)(2)   FLAGGED
  Section 1983 is present in the official
  structural text and does not contain the
  subsection the citation names.
  Release point Online@119-103.

Section 1983 has no subsections at all. A fabricated subsection of a real statute is close to invisible to a human reviewer, because everything before the parenthesis is correct.

*552Rejected

Three checks that would have raised recall, and were not shipped

Each was implemented, measured, and discarded. All three failed the same way: a rare defect on a noisy channel, where a balanced test flatters a check that collapses at the real base rate.

ApproachWould gainMeasured outcome
Flag a pincite when the proposition is absent from the cited page 46 Balanced sample read 77% recall / 77% specificity. At the true base rate — 29 wrong pincites against 562 sound ones — it produces 128 false accusations for 22 findings. 14.9% precision.
Flag a quotation against a fixed threshold 42 A correct quotation matches its source at a median of 0.929, not 1.00, because both the brief and the archive are scanned text. Misquotes sit at 0.719. Every threshold was swept: precision peaks at 17.2%. Still rejected — but see below.
Flag a short-form name matching neither party of the record 4 40% precision — and the false alarms were cases where lookup landed on the wrong record, not where the brief erred.
A bounded “is this still good law” signal — Re-measured with an archive credential. Proximity search is 66× better than the document-level version that was rejected before — and still does not separate. Roe (overruled) returns 46 hits; Twombly (good law) returns 45. The query cannot tell “X overruled Y” from “Y overruled X”, courts overrule objections, and an old case’s rate is diluted by decades of citations predating the overruling.

About 105 of the remaining misses sit behind measured rejections rather than unbuilt work.

One of them was revisited, and the recorded reason was wrong

The note on file said misquote detection failed because the archive text was scanned, and that publisher-quality text would fix it. Re-running the sweep on 236 opinion texts the archive itself marks as not scanned settled that, and not in the expected direction: the scanned and non-scanned subsets behave almost identically. The archive's scanning flag is not the variable, and the planned fix would not have worked.

What decides the outcome is how far this document and the archive disagree — which no fixed threshold can know, and which the document itself carries the evidence to measure. If nine of a brief's quotations match their sources at 0.999 and the tenth matches at 0.977, the tenth is anomalous for this document.

Misquote detectionFixed thresholdCalibrated to the document
Precision17.2%80–91%
Recall—35–56%
Documents too noisy to judgejudged anywaydeclined

It ships as REVIEW and never as a finding. The evidence is synthetic disagreement rather than real scanned briefs, and an anomaly in agreement is not contradiction. The honest sentence is “this quotation matches its source less well than the rest of yours do — read it”, which is a prompt to look, not an accusation.

*553Validation

Citations courts actually sanctioned

Every figure above comes from one benchmark of injected defects. So: 633 citations that courts adjudicated to be fabricated, drawn from a public database of sanctions decisions, provenance pinned by hash. The labels are judicial findings, not an annotator’s judgment.

On 484 scored citationsresult
Flagged239 — 49.4%
Vendor-only identifiers, all reported UNVERIFIED149 / 149
Cost of the entire run4 requests

49.4% is a floor, not recall

The corpus labels a record, not always the citation extracted from it. A narrative reading “counsel cited X, which does not exist; the correct case was Y” can yield Y. Others are real cases carrying a fabricated quotation, which no citation string can reveal.

Every kind of noise pushes the number down, never up. Measured rather than asserted: excluding all 51 rows whose narrative discusses a correction moves it only to 49.7%.

The result that matters more

149 of the 633 are vendor-only identifiers — 2019 WL 1396975 and its kind, real decisions no free archive holds.

Every one was reported UNVERIFIED. None was flagged — on citations that really were fabricated, with a court order to prove it.

That also sets a ceiling worth stating plainly: 23.5% of the defective citations in that corpus are ones this tool will never flag, by design. A checker that flagged them would score better here and be a worse tool.

What it found that the benchmark could not

Asking why 49 citations came back VERIFIED exposed a real defect. The case-name matcher scored State v. Pune against State v. Ing at 1.00, and would confirm a fabricated case name against any unrelated decision printed at the same page whenever the two shared a generic party — State v., People v., Commonwealth v., United States v., In re. That is an enormous share of American case law.

The benchmark could not have found it. Its citations carry years and courts, which the metadata stage uses to disambiguate; strip those, as a bare citation in a sanctions order does, and the case name is doing all the work alone. It was a recall defect rather than a false-accusation one — which is exactly why it survived, in a codebase whose tests attack false accusation hardest.

Fixed and locked under test. The fix moved this corpus from 46.7% to 49.4%, with the vendor-only result unchanged at 149 of 149. This is what external validation is for, and it is the most useful thing the corpus has produced.

This corpus is built from court orders, not from the filings that contained the citations. It answers “given a citation a court adjudicated to be fabricated, is it flagged?” — not “does this catch bad citations in a real brief.” Those are different questions, and the limitations are listed in full before any result in the repository.

*554Pipeline

The checks, in order

A later stage may downgrade a verdict. Nothing can upgrade one. The order matters, so it is numbered — unlike everything else on this page.

01IngestPDF, DOCX, Markdown or text, preserving character offsets so every finding points at a real location.
02ExtractCitations, short forms and their antecedents, quotations, and the proposition each citation supports.
03ResolveAgainst two public archives. Parallel reporters are followed when one archive lacks a series.
04VerifyReporter, existence, case name, court, year, precedential status, quotation, attribution, pincite, proposition.
05ReportA Markdown evidence package and a JSON ledger. Every finding carries retrieved text and a timestamp.

*555Use

Putting it to work

Install and run

# 1. Python 3.10+ required — check yours
python3 --version

# 2. Install into an isolated environment
python3 -m venv verascite-env
source verascite-env/bin/activate
pip install "verascite[all] @ \
  git+https://github.com/rakib-nyc/verascite.git"

# 3. Confirm
verascite --help

# 4. Run it
verascite brief.pdf --out ./audit

Accepts .pdf, .docx, .md and .txt. Writes audit/report.md (the evidence package) and audit/ledger.json. Exit code is 2 when anything is flagged, so it drops into CI unchanged.

No credentials needed. It runs against the public-domain archive out of the box. A free CourtListener token in COURTLISTENER_API_TOKEN widens coverage of recent decisions; it never reaches reports, ledgers, cache keys, or logs — enforced by test. Full install guide, including Windows →

In Python

from pathlib import Path
from verascite.run_audit import audit_document

ledger = audit_document(
    Path("brief.pdf"),
    out_dir=Path("./audit"),
    offline=False,
)

for entry in ledger:
    if entry.overall.value == "FLAGGED":
        print(entry.citation.raw_text)

Inside an AI assistant

VeraScite is built to be called by an AI system as a verification step. The contract is strict for one reason: a caller that renders absence as fabrication turns a neutral observation into a false accusation.

Required of any caller

1. Never render UNVERIFIED as fabricated, fake, or hallucinated.
2. Never upgrade a verdict.
3. Quote the retrieved evidence, not the conclusion.
4. Never present output as a certification or as legal advice.

# tool definition
{
  "name": "verify_citations",
  "description": "Verify legal citations
    against public caselaw archives. IMPORTANT:
    UNVERIFIED means not found in the sources
    consulted — it does NOT mean the citation
    is fabricated.",
  "input_schema": {
    "type": "object",
    "properties": {
      "path":    {"type": "string"},
      "offline": {"type": "boolean"}
    },
    "required": ["path"]
  }
}

Where it fits

  • Pre-filing review. An associate runs it before the partner reads the draft. Flagged items arrive with the retrieved opinion text attached, so confirming a finding takes seconds rather than a database session.
  • Reviewing AI-assisted work product. A research memo comes back perfectly formatted. VeraScite separates “this reporter series has never existed” from “this decision is too recent for free archives.”
  • Opposing filings. Run the deterministic layer alone. Every verdict is reproducible from the same inputs, which matters if you intend to raise the issue with the court.
  • Continuous checking. Fail the build on contradictions only. A gate that also failed on absence would break every time an author cited something recent.
*556Limits

What this does not do

This is experimental software from an independent research project. It is provided without warranty of any kind and does not constitute legal advice. Human verification by a licensed attorney is required before relying on any output.

  • It is not a citator. It cannot tell you whether an authority is still good law, and does not detect overruled, reversed, vacated, abrogated, or superseded decisions.
  • It certifies nothing. The output is an evidence package, not a clearance. Responsibility under Rule 11 and the applicable rules of professional conduct remains with the filing attorney.
  • Its coverage is bounded. United States authority only. Case law, statutes and regulations are covered; legislative materials and secondary sources route to manual review. Non-US authority is out of scope.
  • Statutory currency is not checked. A statutory answer describes the text currently in force. A provision amended or repealed since the events in issue is still reported as existing.
  • Vendor-only identifiers can never be flagged. Measured against real sanctioned filings, that is 23.5% of defective citations. This is by design and will not change.
  • Grounded reading is not reproducible, and the result depends materially on which model does the reading.
  • It can be wrong. Precision is 86.4% on the benchmark above, which means roughly one finding in seven warrants a closer look before you act on it.
