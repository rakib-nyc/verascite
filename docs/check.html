Free  /  nothing installed  /  no sign-up

See which case is actually reported at each citation.

Paste citations from a research memo, a brief, or an AI chat. This looks each one up in a free public archive and shows you the case that is really printed there — so you can compare it against the name you were given.

That comparison is the point. Only about a tenth of bad citations are invented cases. The commonest failure is a real citation attached to the wrong case name, and it survives every check you can do by eye.

One per line, or paste a paragraph. Reporter citations like 550 U.S. 544 or 535 F.3d 225 are what this can find.

What leaves your browser: only the citation strings themselves — never the surrounding text, never your question, never your draft. Lookups go directly from your browser to the CourtListener public API. There is no server in between and nothing is stored.

What this page cannot tell you

  • Whether a citation is fake. It never says that, and neither should anything else. A great many real cases are absent from free archives — recent decisions, unpublished dispositions, state trial courts, and anything carried only by a paid service. Roughly one citation in ten in a real brief is missing from these sources and is perfectly sound.
  • Whether the case says what you were told it says. This checks the address, not the contents. A real case cited for a proposition it does not contain is the single largest category of citation defect and it is invisible here.
  • Whether the quotation is real, or whether the page number is right.
  • Whether the case is still good law. Nothing free can tell you that.
  • United States authority only. This is not legal advice and it certifies nothing.

For quotations, page numbers, and whether an authority supports the proposition it is cited for, run the full tool on the document. It is free and open source, and it runs on your own machine.
